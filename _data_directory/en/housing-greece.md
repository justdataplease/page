description_detailed: "The Bank of Greece apartment price index (2007 = 100) every quarter from 2006 to the second quarter of 2026, for the whole country, Athens, Thessaloniki, the other large cities and the remaining areas, separately for all, new (up to 5 years old) and old apartments, with the change on the previous quarter and on the same quarter a year earlier; the historical series for urban areas go back to 1993. Alongside it are the ECB residential property price index for Greece and the euro area, the interest rate on new mortgages and the stock and growth of housing loans every month (ECB), the HICP rents index, and, from the EU-SILC survey for the 27 EU countries, how many households spend more than 40% of their income on housing, how many own or rent and how many live in overcrowded homes (2003–2025). In the second quarter of 2026 apartment prices were 5.5% higher than a year earlier across the country (Athens 5.0%, Thessaloniki 4.7%, other areas 7.1%); in Athens the index reached 118.3, 111% above its low in the first quarter of 2017. In 2025, 26.4% of households in Greece were overburdened by housing costs, the highest share in the EU (EU27: 7.7%)."
description_preprocess: "Eurostat and ECB data are read in full from their APIs at every update, every period, because they are revised. The Bank of Greece publishes the new index by area and age only as PDF: at every update we find the current file of each kind on its page, download it, archive it unchanged (with its SHA-256 fingerprint) and read its tables; if the layout changes, the update stops instead of guessing. Eurostat and ECB values are first kept exactly as they arrived, with all the source codes, and the final table is checked to have exactly the same rows. Cross-check: the Bank of Greece index for the whole country, rebased to 2025, matches the Bank of Greece series on the ECB portal in all 82 quarters of 2006–2026 (largest difference 0.06 points). Fixed check points: Athens, 2007 average = 100; Thessaloniki, first quarter 2006 = 86.7; rate on new mortgages, December 2024 = 3.65%. The Greek series names are our own (the ECB series are named by hand). In the ECB series the country code is replaced by *, so Greece and the euro area are one series with two areas. The Bank of Greece areas are never added up and have no EU counterpart; the euro-area comparison uses the ECB residential property price index (all dwellings, annual change = value / value a year earlier − 1: Greece 5.5%, euro area 4.0% in the second quarter of 2026). Housing-loan growth is the ECB's growth rate, which corrects for write-offs and loan sales, not the plain change in the stock. Gaps: other urban areas stop in 2019, Athens has monthly values only for 1997–2005, and the latest Bank of Greece values are provisional (flag p)."

**NOTE** - Sources: Bank of Greece, bog_apartment_prices (new apartment index by area and age, 2006–) and bog_dwelling_prices_hist (historical series from 1993); ECB, RESR (residential property prices), MIR (interest rates) and BSI (loans); Eurostat, ilc_lvho07a (housing cost overburden), ilc_lvho02 (tenure), ilc_lvho05a (overcrowding) and the CP041 rents index of prc_hicp_minr. “Overburdened by housing costs” means the household spends more than 40% of its disposable income on housing. The rents index covers all rents, old and new; new leases rise faster. Indices on different bases (2007 = 100, 1997 = 100, 2025 = 100) are compared only as changes. Eurostat data may be reused freely with acknowledgement of the source, ECB data likewise with acknowledgement; we translated and rearranged the Eurostat, ECB and Bank of Greece data, and they are not responsible for the changes.
