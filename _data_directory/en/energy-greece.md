description_detailed: "Electricity prices for households and businesses and gas prices for households, every half-year since 2007, with all taxes, without VAT and without any taxes, in euro and in purchasing power standards, for Greece, the EU27, the euro area and the EU countries. For Greece, net electricity generation every month since 2008 by fuel (lignite, natural gas, oil, hydro, wind, solar) and electricity imports and exports, plus annual production since 1990. For the whole EU, the share of renewables in energy, electricity, heating and transport (2004–2025), dependence on energy imports (1990–2024) and, from the EU-SILC survey, how many people cannot keep their home adequately warm and how many are behind on electricity, water or heating bills (2003–2025). In the second half of 2025 a household with medium consumption (2,500–5,000 kWh a year) paid €0.238 per kilowatt-hour for electricity with all taxes, 18% less than the EU27 average (€0.290) and the tenth cheapest price of the 27 countries. In 2025 renewables covered 60.9% of electricity consumption (EU27: 49.9%), while lignite fell from 52% of production in 2008 to 6% in 2024; that same year 18.1% of the population could not keep their home adequately warm, the highest share in the EU (EU27: 8.8%)."
description_preprocess: "The Eurostat tables are read in full from its API at every update, every period, because prices and energy balances are revised. Each value is first kept exactly as it arrived, with all the source codes, and the final table is checked to have exactly the same rows per table; every code we request (consumption band, fuel, tax) must come back, because the API silently ignores codes that do not exist. The Greek names of fuels, bands and taxes are our own. The price is split the way Eurostat publishes it, using differences within the same country and half-year: energy and network = price without taxes; taxes and levies = price without VAT minus price without taxes; VAT = final price minus price without VAT. In 2022 “taxes and levies” in Greece were negative (−€0.097 and −€0.225 per kWh), because they include subsidies. In monthly generation, “other renewables” (biomass and so on) are all renewables minus hydro, wind and solar; fossil fuels and renewables together match the total within 0.02% over twelve months. Twelve-month shares are each fuel's generation divided by total generation over the same 12 months (to July 2026: renewables 49.6%, natural gas 41.5%, lignite 4.8%). Eurostat's renewables share of electricity (60.9% in 2025) is measured on consumption with the method of the renewables directive and is not the same as the share in monthly generation. Lignite is coded C0000 (coal and lignite) in the monthly series and C0220 in the annual one; net imports are imports minus exports. Prices for different consumption bands, tax levels or currencies are never added up. Gaps: Greece has no household electricity price for the first half of 2026 yet, though it has one for gas; Greek gas prices start in the second half of 2012; the monthly breakdown by fuel (with solar) starts in 2017; Cyprus and Malta have no gas network."

**NOTE** - Eurostat tables: nrg_pc_204 (electricity, households), nrg_pc_205 (electricity, businesses), nrg_pc_202 (gas, households), nrg_cb_pem (monthly net generation by fuel), nrg_cb_em (electricity imports and exports), nrg_bal_peh (annual gross production), nrg_ind_ren (renewables share), nrg_ind_id (import dependency), and from EU-SILC ilc_mdes01 and ilc_mdes07, which also belong to the Income & poverty topic. The stats_eu_rank table gives Greece's position in the EU for each series and period. Household prices are for the medium consumption band (DC, 2,500–4,999 kWh a year; for gas D2, 20–199 GJ); half-year prices are averages and do not show monthly tariff changes. In EU-SILC the euro area compares best as EA20, because the changing-composition EA stops there in 2021.
