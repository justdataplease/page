description_detailed: "Unemployment every month (seasonally adjusted, in total, by sex and for under-25s), the employment rate of people aged 20–64 and long-term unemployment every quarter, the job vacancy rate and employment by sector in Greece, for Greece, the EU27, the euro area and the other 26 EU countries; for the 13 regions, unemployment (ages 15–74 and 15–24) and employment every year since 1999. For pay: the minimum wage every half-year in euro and in purchasing power standards (22 countries), the average full-time salary (1995–2024), the net and gross earnings of an average worker, and the labour cost index and hourly labour costs. In August 2026 unemployment in Greece was 7.4% (EU27: 6.1%), the sixth highest of the 27 countries, down from a peak of 28.3% in July 2013; among under-25s it was 15.6%. The minimum wage reached €1,073 a month in the second half of 2026, while the average full-time salary was €17,954 in 2024, 45% of the EU27 figure; in real terms, once rising prices are taken out, it was 28% lower than in 2010."
description_preprocess: "The Eurostat tables are read in full from its API at every update, every period, because monthly unemployment is revised every month and annual pay every year. Each value is first kept exactly as it arrived, with all the source codes, and the final table is checked to have exactly the same rows per table; every code we request must come back, because the API silently ignores codes that do not exist. Fixed check points against published figures: a minimum wage of €968 in the first half of 2025 (€830 × 14 payments / 12) and €1,073 in the second half of 2026; unemployment of 7.4% in August 2026. The Greek names of age groups, sectors and regions are our own. The minimum wage is given, as Eurostat measures it, in 12 monthly payments, so it includes the holiday bonuses. The real wage is our own calculation: (salary in the year / salary in 2010) divided by (average harmonised price index of the year / average of 2010) × 100, only for years with all 12 months of the index; for Greece in 2024 the nominal salary stood at 86.6 and prices at 120.2 (2010 = 100), so the real wage was 72.1, against 104.5 in the EU27. Unemployment and employment rates never add up across age groups or regions. Monthly and quarterly indicators are seasonally adjusted, regional ones are annual and unadjusted, which is why the country's annual unemployment (8.9% in 2025) differs from the latest month. Gaps: the minimum wage exists in only 22 countries (Austria, Denmark, Finland, Italy and Sweden have no statutory minimum) and has no EU average; the average salary runs to 2024; from 2026 sectors follow the new NACE Rev. 2.1 classification in a separate table that does not join the old one; some regional youth values are flagged u (low reliability, e.g. youth unemployment in the North Aegean)."

**NOTE** - Eurostat tables: for work, une_rt_m (monthly unemployment), lfsi_emp_q (employment rate), une_ltu_q (long-term unemployment), jvs_q_r21 (job vacancies), lfsq_egan2 and lfsq_egan21 (employment by sector, Greece only), lfst_r_lfu3rt and lfst_r_lfe2emprt (unemployment and employment by region); for pay, earn_mw_cur (minimum wage), nama_10_fte (average full-time salary), earn_nt_net (net earnings), lc_lci_r2_q (labour cost index) and lc_lci_lev (hourly labour costs). The real wage uses the all-items HICP (see Inflation). The “average full-time salary” is total wages divided by the number of employees in full-time equivalents, from the national accounts; it is not the median wage. ELSTAT collects the Labour Force Survey data for Greece, and we read them as Eurostat distributes them. The stats_eu_rank table gives Greece's position in the EU for each series and period. Eurostat data may be reused freely with acknowledgement of the source; figures that come from ELSTAT are credited to ELSTAT. We translated and rearranged them; Eurostat and ELSTAT are not responsible for the changes.
