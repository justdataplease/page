We set the new cars registered every year, from **Eurostat**, next to the used cars for sale in **our own listings**. They show two very different markets.

- **New cars:** **144,036** passenger cars were registered in 2025. Half of them were **hybrids**, far more than the EU average. Fully **electric** cars were only **6.2%**, against 17.3% in the EU.
- **Used cars:** in September 2026 the median used car in our listings asked **€9,500**, was **11 years old** and had done **132,500 km**. Almost 6 in 10 ran on petrol.
- **The fleet:** cars per inhabitant have reached the EU average, about 6 per 10 inhabitants. Of the 6.2 million passenger cars, only 30,108 are electric.

## Greece prefers hybrids

In 2025 hybrids (petrol and diesel) were **51%** of new cars in Greece, against 33% in the EU. Diesel cars have almost vanished from registrations (3.4%). Fully electric cars lag behind: Greece is 21st in the EU, ahead only of Italy, Czechia, Romania, Bulgaria, Slovakia and Croatia.

## Used cars tell a different story

Of the used cars listed in September 2026, 57% ran on petrol and 28% on diesel. Hybrids were 5%, plug-ins almost 3% and electric cars just over 1%. These are mostly older cars: the median petrol car listed is 16 years old.

The price follows the fuel and the age. The median used hybrid asked €20,500 and was 4 years old.

{% include article-chart.html id="cars-used-price-fuel-2026-09" %}

The median asking price was €1,000 lower than in September 2025, with drops for petrol, diesel and hybrids. But the listings we see change from month to month, so the change shows a trend, not an exact price change.

## What to keep in mind

- Listing prices are asking prices, not sale prices. We keep only undamaged used cars between €500 and €200,000.
- For Greece Eurostat does not publish the whole fleet by fuel: only electric cars, plug-ins and LPG cars.
- Cars by region are counted where they are registered: leasing companies push Attica up.

All the figures are on the [cars dashboard](/en/data/cars/#dashboard).
