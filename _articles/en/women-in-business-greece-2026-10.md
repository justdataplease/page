Among the active businesses in **GEMI**, the General Commercial Registry, women are **33% of owners**, but 23% of managers and **21% of chairs and chief executives**. In SA companies they are 36% of shareholders and 21% of leaders. The gap between sectors is wider still: half the owners in health and education are women, in construction fewer than 1 in 5.

## Owning is not running

The higher the role, the fewer the women. Among businesses where the owners' gender is known, 27% have women owners only, 12% mixed ownership and 61% men only.

In partnerships (OE and EE), women are **43% of the partners who do not manage the company and 26% of those who do**. The largest gap by legal form is in **SA companies: 36% women among recorded shareholders, 21% among leaders**. Limited partnerships (EE) follow, at 35% and 24%. In sole proprietorships the two shares almost coincide, since owner and leader are the same person.

## From 50% in health to 17% in construction

Among active businesses, women are **50% of owners** in health and social care and 50% in education, 23% in IT, 18% in transport and 17% in construction. But health and education together are only 4% of the businesses we examine, while construction and transport are 15%. Trade, with 27% of the businesses, has 36% women among its owners.

Specific activities are even further apart: **66% in nurseries and day care**, 65% in hairdressing and beauty, 59% in clothing retail. In software programming the share is 21%, in electrical installation 6% and in plumbing, heating and air conditioning 6%.

## More women among newer sole proprietorships

Among sole proprietorships founded from 2013 to 2024, women are **39% to 42%** of owners every year. In older ones the share is much lower: 36% for the 2000s, 19% for the 1980s and 5% for the 1970s. In companies, women are around 29–34% of partners and shareholders, whatever year the company was founded. Even in construction the share has risen, from 7% among businesses founded in 1980–84 to 23% among those founded in 2020–24.

{% include article-chart.html id="women-business-new-owners-all-statuses" %}

For older foundings we only see the businesses that still had owner data in January 2025, active or inactive, and their owners at that point, not in the year they were founded.

By region the differences are small: among active businesses the share ranges from 29% in Western Greece to 36% in Eastern Macedonia and Thrace.

## How we measured it

- **Data:** the October 2026 GEMI registry and the January 2025 person snapshot. The chart by founding year includes businesses of every status; sectors, management and regions cover active businesses. Founding-year comparisons stop at 2024, the last full year before the person snapshot. SA companies do not always publish their shareholders.
- **Gender:** inferred from first and last names for 97% of natural persons; in a random check, 116 of 120 inferences were correct. This is an inference from names, not self-reported gender identity. In Rhodope and Xanthi, gender could not be inferred for around one in four people.
- **What we cannot see:** a business can be registered in a woman's name without her running it in practice. The registry shows who is registered, not who decides.
- **Roles:** owners are sole proprietors, partners and recorded shareholders. Leaders are sole proprietors, managers, chairs, chief executives and legal representatives.
- **Percentages:** women's share of the people with inferred gender, not of the capital. Sectors come from the main activity code.

We publish aggregates only, without names or tax numbers. Processing: DataForGreece; GEMI is not responsible for the calculations. Detailed figures by sector, legal form, region and municipality are on the [dashboard](/en/data/women-in-business/#dashboard).
