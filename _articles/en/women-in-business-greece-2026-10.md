In the active companies of the GEMI registry, **one owner in three is a woman (32.5%)**. The closer we get to management, the lower the share. Women are **23.4%** of managers and only **21.3%** of chairs and chief executives.

## Owners, but not always in charge

Some owners have no role in running the company: partners or shareholders with a stake but no signature. In this group women reach **39.7%**. Among owners who run the company themselves they are 30.5%.

| Role in the company | Women |
|---|---:|
| Owners with no management role | **39.7%** |
| All owners | 32.5% |
| Owners who run the company | 30.5% |
| Managers | 23.4% |
| Chairs and chief executives | **21.3%** |

The gap is widest in partnerships (OE, EE). There, women are 43% of the partners who do not manage and 26% of those who do. 57% of these partnerships have at least one woman partner, but only 8.6% are owned by women alone. One possible explanation is the family business, where a wife or daughter is registered as a partner while someone else runs the firm. The registry cannot prove this, but the picture fits it.

## Two different economies

In health, social care and education **half of all owners are women**. In hairdressing and beauty salons they are 65%, in social work without accommodation 63%.

At the other end are construction (17%), transport (18%) and IT and telecoms (23%). In electrical installation women are 6% of owners. Only **1.7%** of these firms are owned by women alone, and in plumbing, heating and air conditioning 2.1%. In software programming women are 21% of owners.

## The new generation

Sole proprietorships show the change most clearly, because they have a single owner. Of those founded in the 1980s and still active, 23% belong to women. Of those founded in the 2000s, 31%. Of those founded between 2015 and 2024, **38–39%**. One caveat: we only see the businesses that survived, not those that closed in between. Still, the rise is large and steady.

Regions differ little. Women are 28.6% of owners in Western Greece and 36.0% in Eastern Macedonia and Thrace.

## What it means

- Women hold a third of ownership, but a much smaller share of management. The gap is widest in partnerships.
- Sector matters more than region. Trades remain almost entirely male.
- Among new sole proprietorships the share of women keeps rising, but the rise has slowed since 2015.

## How we measured it

- **Gender:** GEMI lists the people of every company and their roles, but not their gender. We inferred it from the first name and the surname ending, which in Greek differs for men and women. We found a gender for 96.6% of individuals. In a random check of 120 people, 116 were right.
- **Owners:** the sole proprietor, and the partners or shareholders listed in the registry. **Runs the company:** a sole proprietor, manager, chair, chief executive or legal representative.
- **Shares:** women as a share of the people whose gender we know, in active companies.
- **Caveats:** the people are as they stood in the registry in January 2025, so companies founded later do not count yet. Public limited companies (AE) do not always publish their shareholders.

We do not publish names or tax numbers of individuals, only totals. We processed the GEMI data ourselves and GEMI is not responsible for the calculations. Every number in this article is in the [dashboard](/en/data/women-in-business/#dashboard), by sector, legal form and region.
