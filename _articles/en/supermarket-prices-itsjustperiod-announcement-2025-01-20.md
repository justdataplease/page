In Greece, period products such as pads and tampons carry 24% VAT, the same rate as luxury goods like cigarettes and cosmetics. This high tax makes these basic hygiene products too expensive for many families.

Imagine hundreds of young girls who cannot go to school because their families cannot afford basic products. Their mothers, who already struggle to buy food, have to choose between milk and pads. That hard choice is real for many families in the country.

Or, in numbers, from a Market Research Bureau survey (October 2018):
- 18% of mothers say they have skipped buying basic goods at least once in order to buy pads. That is a full Odeon of Herodes Atticus, not once but 28 times over.
- More than 10,000 girls have missed at least one day of school because they could not afford period products.
- 20% of mothers in Greece say they struggle to afford period products. It would take about 4,840 thirty-seat buses to carry all of these women.

To change this, the **It’s Just Period** team is working to cut or abolish the tax on period products, and runs awareness campaigns to explain why the change is needed.

Together with **Data for Greece**, they track the prices of these products, so that if the tax is cut, shoppers get the reduction and the supermarkets do not keep it, as has happened many times elsewhere in Europe.
