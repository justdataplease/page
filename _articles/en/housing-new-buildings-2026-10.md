We set **ELSTAT**'s building permits next to **Eurostat**'s construction costs and prices of new homes, and to the asking prices in the property sale listings we collect.

- **Permits:** in the 12 months to June 2026, **31,423** private building permits were issued, 10.4% more than in the 12 months before. That is still less than half the 2007 level (79,407).
- **Costs:** building a new home costs **28%** more than in 2021, while the prices buyers pay the builders rose 20%.
- **Listings:** homes built in 2015 or later ask a median **€4,000 per m²**, 82% more than homes built before 1980 (€2,195).

## Permits are rising, but from very low

During the crisis building permits fell from 79,407 in 2007 to 12,641 in 2016. Since then they have risen almost every year, to 30,678 in 2024. 2025 was slightly lower (29,940), but the first half of 2026 had 15,068 permits, 11% more than the first half of 2025.

Construction output tells the same story: in the second quarter of 2026 its index reached 161.9 (2021 = 100), against 104.9 in the EU. It is still below a third of its 2007 level.

## Building got dearer faster than prices

From 2015 to 2020 the cost of building a home, materials and labour, barely moved. Since 2021 it has climbed steeply: in the second quarter of 2026 it was 28% above 2021 and 4.1% above a year earlier. The prices buyers pay the builders rose less, 20% since 2021.

## In the listings, a new home costs almost twice as much

About one in five sale listings that give a construction year is for a home built in 2015 or later. In 9 of the 12 regions with enough listings, new homes ask more than twice as much as homes built before 1980. In the Peloponnese and the North Aegean they ask two and a half times as much. The smallest gap is in Central Macedonia: €2,979 against €2,333 per m².

{% include article-chart.html id="housing-newbuild-asking-regions" %}

The most expensive new homes are in the South Aegean (€5,245 per m²) and Attica (€4,538), where they ask almost twice the price of old homes.

## What to keep in mind

- Listing prices are asking prices, not sale prices. New homes also differ from old ones in other ways, such as location and size.
- ELSTAT's building permits cover all private buildings, not only homes.
- Eurostat's construction cost index covers new residential buildings. The EU no longer publishes an average, so we show it for Greece only.

All the figures, by month and quarter, are on the [housing dashboard](/en/data/housing/#dashboard), page "Νέα κτίρια".
