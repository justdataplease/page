In this article we look at one popular budget product, Minerva olive oil (1 litre), and how its price moved in two large supermarket chains in Greece.<br>
The chart below shows the olive oil price on the Greek market from January 2022 to January 2025.

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/{{ item.image_path | default: 'assets/posts/supermarket-prices-olive-oil-2025-01.png' }}" class="img-fluid w-100 h-100" alt="">

The picture is typical of the crisis that hit the olive oil market: prices stayed fairly stable through 2022, between 6 and 7 euros per litre.
From mid-2023, however, they climbed sharply, doubled and peaked in January 2024 at 14 euros per litre.

Average olive oil consumption in Greece is <a href="https://www.olivenews.gr/el/diatrofi/koyzina-estiasi/ellada-echase-tin-1i-thesi-stin-kefali-katanalosi-elaioladoy/">[1]</a> 11.22 litres per person a year,
so the rise from 6€ to 14€ means about 90€ more a year for the average consumer, if consumption stays the same.

After the peak prices eased somewhat, but they are still high, at 11 to 12 euros per litre in the chains we looked at.
<br>To get back to the pre-crisis level of 2022, they still have a long way to fall.

When we add more supermarket chains and other 1-litre olive oils of different brands and qualities, we see <strong>the same easing</strong>.

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/{{ item.image_path | default: 'assets/posts/supermarket-prices-olive-oil-2025-01_02.png' }}" class="img-fluid w-100 h-100" alt="">

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/{{ item.image_path | default: 'assets/posts/supermarket-prices-olive-oil-2025-01_03.png' }}" class="img-fluid w-100 h-100" alt="">

Shelf prices should fall further, since producer prices have already come down. Shoppers will feel the full drop only once the existing stock, bought at higher prices, has sold out.
