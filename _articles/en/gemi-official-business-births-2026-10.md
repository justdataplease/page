We set the new businesses registered each year in **GEMI**, the General Commercial Registry, next to the official **enterprise births** from **Eurostat**, which come from ELSTAT's business register. They do not count exactly the same thing. The official figures count every business that started an activity, including self-employed professionals, who do not register with GEMI. The gap between the two shows where the registry sees well and where it does not.

- **Overall:** **88,028** new businesses started in 2024 according to Eurostat. GEMI registered **67,103** in the same sectors, about 3 in 4.
- **By sector:** almost all of them in accommodation, food service and trade. In health, only 1 in 3.
- **Speed:** the official figures stop at 2024. GEMI already has 2025: **74,031** new businesses in the same sectors, 10% more than in 2024.

## Trade and tourism in, self-employed professionals out

In accommodation and food service GEMI recorded **99%** of the new businesses of 2024, and in trade **98%**. In health it recorded only **34%**, in education 46% and in professional and scientific activities 49%. Doctors, lawyers, engineers and accountants register with the tax office without going through GEMI. Coverage is also low in transport (59%).

In three sectors GEMI has **more** new companies than the official count, about 1.3 times as many: energy, finance and real estate. These are most likely companies that do not start a normal activity straight away, such as holding companies or companies set up for a single solar park. The official statistics count a business only once it has turnover or staff.

## The official figures come later

Since 2021 new registrations in GEMI have risen every year. The official figures jump more: **90,224** births in 2022 and **78,754** in 2023. Official business **deaths** doubled, from 18,687 in 2021 to 37,391 in 2024. The 2024 figure is still provisional.

## By region

The official figures by region stop at 2023. In the South Aegean GEMI recorded **92%** of the new businesses, in the Ionian Islands 81% and in Attica 79%. In Western Macedonia, only **57%**. Western Macedonia also has the highest death rate: 4.7 businesses close for every 100 active, against 3 in Attica.

## What to keep in mind

- GEMI counts registrations by year of incorporation. Eurostat counts businesses that started an activity in the year. The ratio shows coverage, not exactly which businesses are missing.
- We compare the same sectors: industry, construction and market services, without agriculture, public administration and membership organisations. We count head offices only, not branches.
- The official figures are revised. Deaths for the latest year are provisional.

All the figures, by sector and region, are on the page «ΓΕΜΗ και επίσημα στοιχεία» of the [businesses dashboard](/en/data/businesses/#dashboard).
